<p>It is 9:12 pm here in Ithaca, New York. I just finished sending a
voice memo to my partner about <em>Ribes</em>. When I get talking about
gooseberries I can help but think about <em>Ribes aureum</em>. One of
the first scientific names I had down pat. This species is planted all
around Colorado College’s campus and one of the first plants I was
blessed to learn in field botany.</p>
<p><img src="{{ site.url }}{{ site.baseurl }}/images/ribes-disc.jpg"
alt="ribes aureum at the aviary disc golf course (inaturalist.org/observations/158628931)" />
I do not mean to go on a tangent about <em>Ribes</em> so I will leave it
at that for now. I really wanted to make a blog post because I think the
only person who reads this blog is my friend David He always gets on me
for not posting on here enough so this one is for you.</p>
<p><img src="{{ site.url }}{{ site.baseurl }}/images/flynnsgiving-2025.jpeg"
alt="its becoming a tradition" /> I feel like an update on my graduate
studies is due. So much has happened since my last post on August 27th,
2025. I have completed field work in Florida, Belize, Texas and Panama
in that order and in a week will be heading to Honduras to visit the
Paul Stanley Herbarium and geek out and collect some
<em>Catopsis</em>.</p>
I am really blessed to be supported by a variety of grants and more
importantly an amazing community of folks who unbeknownst have become
the energy of a wave to keep me going in my studies. I want to thank
Cole Luttrel, Ella Baron and Ramon daPena for their support logistically
in the field. On top of that, I must say thanks for the laughter and fun
while out looking for <em>Catopsis.</em>

<p>I am feeling pretty emotional right now, for better or for worse, I
have been going about my work and life in silence. My emotions have felt
to heavy to listen to music, and the feelings and focus I have had
during those moments without music has slowly crept into my time in the
lab.</p>
<p>So really why am I writing this blog post right now? Because I wanted
to write down that I shed a good amount of tears looking at photos of my
late cat Buddy Guy. The tears came from this feeling of wishing I had
the emotional intimacy I am bulding right now which I only can hope
would have made me a better owner (who did not pick him up as much when
we first got him). I have done my share of looking back at old photos
trying to find similar photographs like above of <em>Ribes aureum.</em>
All that said, now in my 4th year of my phd I feel old, I feel happy
that the hortorium is in a renaissance, I feel wise and sad that I wish
I had the emotional intimacy as I think I could have tackled so many
more additional things during my phd if I had realized this sooner. I
still have time so I am happy it occurred this August to the present
day.</p>
